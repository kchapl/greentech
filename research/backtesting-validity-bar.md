# Validity bar for daily equity backtests

**Research ticket:** [Establish the validity bar for backtesting daily equity strategies](https://github.com/kchapl/greentech/issues/12)  
**Map:** [greentech: spec for a mirror-and-forecast green-tech investing app](https://github.com/kchapl/greentech/issues/9)  
**Accessed:** 2026-09-28  
**Scope:** Methodological controls for historical daily-equity tests of this single-user, read-only signal app. UK/European listings are primary and US listings secondary. This is not a strategy recommendation or an assertion that a valid backtest predicts future profit.

## Finding

A daily strategy backtest is not credible evidence if it can see information before it was available, tests only securities that survived to the present, mishandles corporate actions or delistings, assumes impossible fills, omits material costs, or selects the winning rule after trying many alternatives without accounting for that search.

At minimum, an evaluation intended to support a forecasting signal should:

1. **Use point-in-time information.** At every decision timestamp, calculate the signal only from observations and records that would actually have been available then. Define the market-session timezone and when a completed daily bar becomes usable. A rule computed from the close of day *t* cannot also assume a fill at that same close unless the order timing and information flow make that fill possible. For non-price inputs, preserve an availability/publication timestamp (not just the period the data describes) and apply realistic reporting/revision lags. QuantConnect's time model describes presenting events in order of arrival; this is a useful event-time invariant, not independent validation of any dataset.
2. **Use a survivorship-safe, historically valid universe.** Reconstruct which securities were eligible at each historical date using only contemporaneously available membership/instrument information, or clearly limit the claim to a fixed, predeclared universe and state the resulting limitation. Include dead, renamed, acquired, suspended, and delisted names when relevant; do not silently drop failed securities or truncate their returns at the last convenient quote. Shumway documents that negative delistings can be surprises and that missing delisting returns in CRSP were large, illustrating why surviving-name-only samples can bias results.
3. **Make price, corporate-action, and return accounting consistent.** Specify whether input prices are raw, split-adjusted, or total-return/dividend-adjusted, and keep indicator inputs, execution prices, position quantities, dividend cash flows, and reported returns consistent with that choice. Avoid both unadjusted split discontinuities and look-ahead from retrospectively adjusted histories used as if the adjusted value had been known in the past. Account for splits, cash distributions, symbol changes, mergers and delistings, or document explicitly what the data cannot represent. LEAN's documentation illustrates the necessary event classes (splits, dividends, symbol changes and delistings) and split/normalization considerations; provider-specific semantics still need verification for each chosen UK/EU and US feed.
4. **Model an executable daily decision and fills.** State when a signal is formed, when an order could first be submitted, and what subsequent price/market session is used for a fill. Daily OHLC bars do not reveal the intraday path or queue position; do not claim fills at a bar's high/low or at a known close without a defensible execution rule. Include spread and slippage/market impact appropriate to liquidity and order size, and avoid trading unavailable volume. QuantConnect defines slippage as expected versus actual fill price and notes that modeling it makes backtests more realistic. A zero-slippage assumption is a limitation, not a neutral default.
5. **Subtract applicable trading frictions and report net results.** Include commissions and exchange/broker fees, bid-ask spread, slippage/market impact, and applicable taxes/duties for the tested market, instrument, and route. UK SDRT is generally 0.5% for electronic CREST share purchases according to GOV.UK, but exemptions and different transaction/settlement routes exist; do not apply a blanket UK tax rate to every UK/EU listing or assume the duty is charged on every instrument/trade. QuantConnect's transaction-fee documentation demonstrates that fee schedules are a distinct model from fills/slippage. Show gross and net results if useful, but evaluate the claimed edge net of the declared costs.
6. **Keep model selection separate from final evaluation.** Record the strategies, parameters, universes, and variants tried. Use chronological train/validation/test separation or a justified walk-forward procedure; freeze choices before looking at the final holdout, and do not repeatedly tune against it. For overlapping labels/holding periods or dependent observations, use a split/inference procedure that respects time and dependence rather than random shuffling. Walk-forward is not magic: any repeated parameter selection still belongs to the research search and must be disclosed. Sullivan, Timmermann and White apply a bootstrap Reality Check to a full universe of technical rules to account for data-snooping; Harvey, Liu and Zhu describe why repeated factor discovery raises the significance hurdle. Bailey et al. propose PBO/CSCV as one framework for diagnosing selection overfit, while noting ordinary holdout methods can be unreliable in investment simulations. These methods are alternatives to consider, not mandatory product requirements established by this ticket.
7. **Report uncertainty, coverage, and limitations rather than only a winning equity curve.** State sample dates, markets, symbols/universe construction, data vendor/adjustment semantics, missing-data handling, rebalance and execution assumptions, all cost assumptions, and number of strategy variants examined. Report net performance alongside benchmark-relative performance and risk/drawdown/turnover/trade count, with uncertainty or sensitivity analysis appropriate to the design. A short or narrow sample and unstable results should be presented as weak evidence, not concealed by a point estimate.

These controls make a result more interpretable; they do **not** establish that a signal will make money, nor do they eliminate regime change, data errors, model misspecification, or uncertainty about future execution. The mirror's observed market state and a forecast signal must remain visually and semantically distinct.

## What this ticket does not decide

The methodological minimum does not set the product's numeric promotion threshold: required holdout length, minimum trade count, acceptable drawdown, confidence level, benchmark, cost stress cases, market/regime coverage, or whether an out-of-sample result is strong enough to show a signal as actionable. Those are explicit choices for [Set the evidence bar the forecasting half must clear](https://github.com/kchapl/greentech/issues/17), which asks over what period, against what benchmark, and net of what costs. Do not convert cited academic significance thresholds into product cutoffs without deciding the design and applicability.

Data availability and legal right to retain/display provider data are separate from statistical validity. UK/EU provider coverage and exchange-specific corporate-action semantics remain open in [provider comparison #21](https://github.com/kchapl/greentech/issues/21), and usage/display terms remain open in [rights assessment #20](https://github.com/kchapl/greentech/issues/20). For price-only signals, the research lag question can be scoped to bar availability; if later signals consume fundamentals/news/ESG information, those records need point-in-time vintage and publication-time handling as well.

## Primary and authoritative sources

1. QuantConnect, [Timeslices / time modeling](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/key-concepts/time-modeling/timeslices) — describes presenting data events in arrival order. Used as an example of event-time modeling, not a universal standard.
2. QuantConnect, [US equity corporate actions](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/securities/asset-classes/us-equity/corporate-actions) — documents split, dividend, symbol-change and delisting notifications and split/normalization behavior. Its details are US-feed-specific; confirm other exchanges and vendors separately.
3. Tyler Shumway, [“The Delisting Bias in CRSP Data” (1997), BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9278/) — peer-reviewed paper abstract reports missing/incorrect returns for many negative delistings and large omitted delisting returns.
4. QuantConnect, [Slippage key concepts](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/slippage/key-concepts) — defines slippage as the difference between expected and actual fill price and explains its role in more realistic tests.
5. QuantConnect, [Transaction-fee key concepts](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/transaction-fees/key-concepts) — documents fee models as a separate component of backtest reality modeling.
6. HM Revenue & Customs, [Tax when you buy shares: overview](https://www.gov.uk/tax-buy-shares) and [buying shares electronically](https://www.gov.uk/tax-buy-shares/buy-shares-electronically) — describes general 0.5% SDRT/Stamp Duty treatment, electronic CREST SDRT, and exemptions/route distinctions. Applies to relevant UK transactions, not universally across the app's universe.
7. QuantConnect, [Walk-forward optimization](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/optimization/walk-forward-optimization) — describes periodic adjustment using a trailing window; included as an implementation example, not a claim that a walk-forward design alone removes overfit.
8. Robert Sullivan, Allan Timmermann and Halbert White, [“Data Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap” (1998), LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119144/) — abstract describes testing the full universe of examined technical rules with White's Reality Check bootstrap to quantify data-snooping effects.
9. Campbell R. Harvey, Yan Liu and Heqing Zhu, [“… and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns,” NBER Working Paper 20592](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592) — discusses extensive factor data mining and multiple-testing significance thresholds. Its proposed research threshold is not adopted here as an app acceptance cutoff.
10. David H. Bailey, Jonathan Borwein, Marcos López de Prado and Qiji Jim Zhu, [“The Probability of Backtest Overfitting,” WMU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/42/) — abstract proposes PBO and combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, and cautions that standard holdout methods may be unreliable for investment simulations.
